About

Who I am

I'm an undergraduate computer engineering student at George Washington University with a deep focus on robotics, computer vision, and applications of Reinforcement Learning to games. My work spans the full stack — from training neural networks to wiring servos and electrical components.

Currently, I work in the Intelligent Aerospace Systems Lab (IASL) at GWU, where my current research focuses on safe autonomous landing for UAS systems. My paper, "Open-Vocabulary UAS Delivery Zone Obstruction Detection via Fine-Tuned YOLO-World" is planned to be submitted to the AIAA SciTech Forum 2027.

Previously at IASL, I have worked on autonomous vehicle simulations such as CARLA and SUMO, integrated using OpenCDA. As an intern at C-3 Comm Systems LLC, I handled full-stack web development by implementing and maintaining an internal Operations Management System and built autonomous UGV systems powered by Nvidia Jetson. Outside of work, I'm currently building: a 6-DOF robotic arm, RL agents in Minecraft, and custom voice controlled smart home systems.

Publications

Open-Vocabulary Obstruction Detection for UAS Landing via Fine-Tuned YOLO-World

Andre Arante, Mahyar Ghazanfari, Can Vu, Peng Wei

George Washington University · In submission — AIAA SciTech 2027

Safe autonomous landing remains a critical challenge for package-delivery UAS, where an undetected obstruction in the landing zone poses a serious hazard. Traditional closed-set object detectors only recognize the fixed classes they were trained on, so they can't generalize to the arbitrary obstacles found in real-world deployment. We instead propose open-vocabulary detection: fine-tuning YOLO-World on the VisDrone aerial dataset while freezing the CLIP text encoder to preserve zero-shot generalization to novel obstacle types queryable in natural language.
