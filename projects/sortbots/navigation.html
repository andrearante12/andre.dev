Chasing the edge of the known.

Nobody hands the fleet a map. Each robot drives at the edge of what it has already seen, stands back, and looks — planning against a live grid, on frontiers that any robot has revealed. Most goals never formally arrive; giving up well is the job.

Turn, then go

The base can physically strafe; the navigator will not let it, because the only obstacle sensor points where the robot faces — travelling sideways is driving blind. Nav2 runs MPPI with a differential-drive motion model, scoring two thousand candidate rollouts per cycle against a 2.8 s horizon. There is no map_server and no amcl: RTAB-Map and the fleet map merger own the map between them.

A reactive behaviour tree checks the current path against the costmap at 4 Hz and replans immediately when a lethal cell lands on it — worst-case reaction to a newly-blocked path drops from ~2 s to ~0.5 s. Unknown cells don't invalidate a path, so frontier goals through unmapped space don't cause replan thrash.

Frontier exploration

explorer.py clusters frontiers — free cells bordering unknown space — on the fused /map, scores them by size, openness and distance, and drives Nav2 toward the best one. The goal is pulled back into known free floor and posed facing the frontier, so the camera reveals it from a standoff rather than the robot nosing into an unmapped corner. It repeats until no frontier is reachable, then stops.

Genuinely unreachable frontiers — behind a rack, behind sim geometry — used to return on a flat timer forever, which made "exploration done" structurally unreachable. Re-blacklisting near an existing entry now bumps a strike count that grows both its radius and its lifetime; at three strikes it stops expiring.

Two maps of one warehouse

Each robot maps alone — its own RTAB-Map database, its own pose graph, no loop closure between robots. The fleet shares a product, not a process: the bringup publishes a static map → {robot_id}/map transform from each robot's known spawn pose (configs/robots.yaml), and two merge nodes resample every robot's grid and cloud into one world frame. Collaborative fusion, not collaborative SLAM.

Field notes

Diagnosed live · 2026-08-09

Nav2 was severed from the robot and still looked healthy

The collision monitor is the final velocity hop — it republishes cmd_vel_smoothed as cmd_vel. It was missing from the lifecycle manager's node list, and an unmanaged Nav2 lifecycle node stays unconfigured forever, publishing nothing. The robot never moved on a nav command while the controller logged "passing new path to controller" at 4 Hz all run — masked because the recovery behaviours publish cmd_vel directly, so recovery spins did visibly rotate the robot. It must be listed, and listed last.

Diagnosed live · 2026-08-02

The goal checker, not the progress checker

Frontier goals kept timing out within a metre of the target. The suspicion was the progress checker; the cause was the goal tolerance. A frontier goal sits at the edge of known space by definition — a harder final approach than an interior waypoint — and the robot settled ~0.23 m short of a 0.15 m tolerance and sat there until timeout. Widened to 0.25 m in both position and yaw; goals started succeeding immediately.

Field note

Why anchors get their own topic instead of /tf

The anchor transforms were first published on the standard transform topics. The dashboard throttles its transform subscription to one message per 50 ms, and the simulator publishes odometry transforms at ~60 Hz per robot — so a once-a-second anchor essentially never won the throttle window and the browser dropped it forever. Anchors now publish on their own held-and-replayed topic, so a tab opened at any moment receives every current anchor immediately.
